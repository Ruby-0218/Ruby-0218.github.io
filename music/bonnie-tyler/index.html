Where I heard it

First heard this in a Modern Family episode where the family hangs out on a houseboat for a solar eclipse. Jay gets all reflective about legacy and being remembered, but it ends in a wholesome family sing-along.

Best setting

Late-night road trips, or whenever you’re having a mild existential crisis and need a dramatic soundtrack.

Fun fact

“Total Eclipse of the Heart” is pure 80s theatrical gold. It’s dramatic, unfiltered, and practically forces you to perform a passionate lip-sync in your bedroom. It fully embraces the “more is more” philosophy, and there’s a pretty wild reason for that.

Songwriter Jim Steinman originally penned this track for a Dracula musical. It perfectly explains why the whole song is dripping with gothic drama and why there’s a literal line about “creatures of the night.” Also, that haunting, totally brain-washing “Turn around, bright eyes” echoing in the background? That actually belongs to Canadian rock singer Rory Dodd.

But the best part is the cosmic-level free marketing. Every time a real-life solar eclipse happens, this song’s Spotify streams skyrocket by up to 3,000%. Bonnie Tyler even performed it live on a cruise ship during the 2017 Great American Eclipse, singing right to the actual astronomical event.

Lesson learned: If you want to create a song that lives forever, just write a dramatic vampire anthem and let the solar system handle the marketing for you ;)
