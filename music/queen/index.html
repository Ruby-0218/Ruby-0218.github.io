Fun fact

Some classic songs take months of painstaking polish, but “Under Pressure” was born out of pure, late-night, wine-fueled chaos. I’ve always been so jealous of these geniuses—and I love them for it.

Back in 1981, David Bowie casually dropped by Queen’s studio in Switzerland just to hang out. One thing led to another, and after a few drinks and some pizza, they ended up in an wild, impromptu jam session. Bowie even suggested a “blind singing” game where he and Freddie went into the vocal booth separately to improvise without hearing what the other had just sung.

The crazy part? That iconic 7-note bassline almost vanished into thin air. Bassist John Deacon came up with the legendary riff, went out to eat pizza, and completely forgot how it went by the time he came back. Luckily, drummer Roger Taylor remembered the exact rhythm and hummed it back to him before it was lost forever.

Lesson learned: Always record your best ideas before you leave the studio for pizza ;)

Fun fact

There’s something undeniably grand about a full, booming gospel choir - except when you realize Queen never actually hired one for this song…

Freddie Mercury was a huge fan of Aretha Franklin and wanted to create his own rock-gospel masterpiece. But instead of recruiting a real choir, Freddie, Brian May, and Roger Taylor stayed in the studio and overdubbed their own voices over a hundred times. Just three guys, layering their harmonies over and over until they sounded like a massive cathedral choir.

The irony? While the world constantly worships “Bohemian Rhapsody,” Freddie secretly preferred “Somebody to Love” and considered it his finest piece of songwriting. BTW, me too. I prefer this song over BR, so don’t argue with me cuz Freddie’s on my side.

Years after he passed, George Michael took the stage at Freddie’s 1992 tribute concert and sang it with so much raw soul that the live recording instantly topped the UK charts, proving Freddie’s gospel dream was immortal. I guess true greatness is never forgotten.

Lesson learned: You don’t need a 100-person choir to create magic - just three rock legends and endless studio patience ;)

Fun fact

A nearly 6-minute rock-opera suite with zero chorus, lyrics mentioning Scaramouche and Galileo, and a dark murder confession - on paper, this song sounded like commercial suicide.

When Freddie Mercury showed “Bohemian Rhapsody” to record executives, they actually refused to release it as a single, claiming no radio station in the world would ever play a 6-minute track. But guess what? Freddie refused to trim a single second. He gave a tape to his friend, London radio DJ Kenny Everett, with strict instructions not to play it on air. Kenny, knowing a masterwork when he heard one, did the exact opposite—he played it 14 times in a single weekend. Station phones lit up like a Christmas tree, and the record label was forced to yield.

The recording process was just as unhinged. To get that iconic opera section, Freddie, Brian, and Roger stacked their vocals over 180 times using 24-track analog tape. They overdubbed so endlessly that the physical tape literally wore out and turned see-through… gosh!

The irony? The “too long” song stayed at #1 on the UK charts for nine weeks in a row and changed pop music forever. (Felt kind of sorry for other singers btw)

Lesson learned: If everyone tells you your idea is too long and too weird, you’re probably onto something legendary (but if you’re not Freddie, maybe just listen to the professionals) ;)
