Fleetwood Mac

Beautiful emotional chaos

1967–present London, England Rock · Pop rock · Soft rock

The drama behind Fleetwood Mac’s Rumours album is honestly better than most reality TV shows, and “Go Your Own Way” captures that chaotic, toxic energy perfectly. It is loud, bitter, and painfully honest, yet somehow beautifully packaged into a timeless pop-rock masterpiece. It is wild to think about how much pain they were putting each other through in the studio, but it just goes to show that while their romantic relationship was a total disaster, their musical chemistry was an absolute force of nature.

Fun fact

I’ve never been through anything like this myself, but I’ve always felt that tragic love stories have a strange kind of magic. They hurt, sure, but they also leave behind some of the best music ever made.

This song is a proof. Stevie Nicks wrote it after spotting a highway sign reading “Silver Spring, Maryland” while going through a messy breakup with her bandmate, Lindsey Buckingham. To her, it represented a dreamland where their love actually worked out.

The irony? The band cut the track from their iconic album Rumours and buried it on the B-side of “Go Your Own Way” - Lindsey’s own diss track about her. But Stevie left a quiet little promise in her lyrics:

“You’ll never get away from the sound of the woman that loved you.”

Fast forward 20 years to their 1997 reunion concert. Mid-song, Stevie turned around, looked Lindsey dead in the eye, and sang those exact words straight to his face while he had to keep playing guitar and singing harmony. Talk about an elite level of pettiness.

Lesson learned: If you’re going to break up, maybe don’t do it with someone holding the microphone ;)

Best setting

Fun fact

“Go Your Own Way” is basically the ultimate rock and roll breakup anthem, written by Lindsey Buckingham directly at Stevie Nicks while they were still in the same band. Their relationship was crumbling under the weight of massive fame, and instead of talking it out calmly, Lindsey channeled all his anger and resentment into this aggressively upbeat, catchy track.

The most brutal part of the song is the line “Packing up, shacking up is all you wanna do.” Stevie was absolutely furious about it. She felt it falsely painted her as someone just sleeping around, and she constantly begged Lindsey to take that line out. He stubbornly refused, knowing exactly how much it got under her skin. Stevie later admitted she wanted to go over and kill him every time she had to sing it on stage.

To make matters even more awkward, Stevie actually had to sing backup vocals on this very track, helping to harmonize on her own diss track. Underneath all this musical tension, Stevie always felt that a lot of Lindsey’s bitterness came from pure jealousy. She was rapidly becoming the undeniable star and face of Fleetwood Mac, a reality he just could not stand.

Lesson learned: Never break up with a songwriter, because they will write a catchy hit about you and force you to sing backup on it for the rest of your life ;)
