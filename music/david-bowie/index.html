David Bowie

The ultimate chameleon of rock and pop

1962–2016 Brixton, London, England Art rock · Glam rock · Pop · Electronic

David Bowie is one of those artists who didn't just make music; he created entire galaxies. He was the ultimate chameleon. Even when you are just listening to an audio track, you can feel the distinct persona, the theatricality, and the sheer avant-garde genius bleeding through.

There’s a strange, magnetic power in his music. Bowie didn’t just sing songs; he invited us to step into his weird, wonderful universe. He made it perfectly okay to be an alien, reminding us that being different is exactly what makes you legendary, and always make me think that sometimes being different doesn’t mean you are a freak, you are just unique.

Fun fact

I love how Bowie never just released music; he created entire alternate universes.

For this album, he didn’t just sing - he transformed into Ziggy Stardust, an androgynous, bisexual alien rockstar who fell to Earth. Back in 1972, being that openly avant-garde and unapologetic about his identity was incredibly shocking. He boldly announced his sexuality in interviews without any shyness, completely defying the conservative norms of the time.

The craziest part? “Starman” was actually a last-minute addition to the album because the record label complained they didn’t have a hit single. Bowie casually wrote this masterpiece, went on national TV, pointed right at the camera during the chorus, and completely melted the brains of teenagers everywhere.

Lesson learned: If you’re going to be weird, don’t just be a little weird - be a full-blown alien rockstar and own it ;)

Fun fact

It takes a special kind of dark humor to write a song about a disaster, and then watch the whole world use it to celebrate a triumph.

Bowie released this track in 1969, just days before the historic Apollo 11 moon landing. The song sounded so brilliantly futuristic that the BBC actually used it as the background music for their live lunar landing broadcast.

The irony? If you actually listen to the lyrics, the song is literally about an astronaut (Major Tom) experiencing a catastrophic malfunction, severing ties with Earth, and floating away to die alone in the abyss of space. The BBC apparently didn’t pay attention to the lyrics before playing it for millions of anxious viewers watching actual astronauts walk on the moon.

Lesson learned: Always double-check the lyrics before you pick the soundtrack for a historic space mission ;)
